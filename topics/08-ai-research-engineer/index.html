The LLM research-engineer roadmap: 12 stages, from autograd to open-source teardowns

A popular 12-stage plan for becoming an AI research engineer. Each stage gets its own page: the concepts explained, a project you can actually run, a reality check, links to where this course already covers it, and the real courses that teach it (course map).

Source: Suraj Sharma, X post, 21 Oct 2026 · saved in sources/08-suraj-ai-research-engineer.md

Start here (no maths needed)

A research engineer turns new AI ideas from papers into working, fast, well-tested models. This roadmap lists the 12 skills they use.

Think of it like this

Think of a Formula 1 team. Some people invent new ideas for the car (researchers). Others build the engine, test it on the track, make it faster and fix what breaks (engineers). A research engineer does a bit of both: reads the new idea, builds it, races it, and reports honestly how it did.

How it works

  1. Know the engine: the maths and code inside a neural network (stage 1).
  2. Feed it well: choose how big to make it and what data to train it on (stages 2 and 4).
  3. Try new designs: new kinds of model, new ways to see pictures and hear sound (stages 3 and 7).
  4. Teach it manners, and make it fast: make it behave well, train it on many computers at once, and make it quick to use (stages 5, 6 and 8).
  5. Look inside, test hard and share: find out how it thinks, attack it to find weak spots, and publish your work (stages 9–12).

Why robots care

Robot "brains" are now built from the same parts. Stage 7, joining a vision model to a language model, is exactly how robots that follow spoken instructions are made. Stage 8, making models fast, decides whether the robot reacts in time.

Words to know

Research engineer
Someone who builds and tests new AI ideas in code.
Training

Inference

GPU

The 12 stages

01

PyTorch internals, hand-written backward passes, Triton kernels, memory layout and strides.

02

Compute-optimal model size, deduplication, domain weights, learning-rate schedules.

03

Mixture of experts, state-space models (Mamba/S4), linear attention, ring attention.

04

Self-instruct, rejection sampling, verifier filtering, training a small model on the survivors.

05

DPO, KTO, ORPO, reward models, and how to stop reward hacking.

06

Data, tensor and pipeline parallelism, ZeRO/FSDP, NCCL, gradient checkpointing.

07

Vision encoders, projection layers, cross-attention, early vs late fusion. The core of a VLA.

08

GQA/MQA, KV-cache size and quantisation, speculative decoding.

09

Logit lens, activation patching, finding the circuit behind a fact.

10

Contamination checks, dynamic evals, calibrated LLM judges, attacking your own models.

11

Reproduce a new paper's core idea in under 500 lines and benchmark it honestly.

12

Model cards, public training logs, and honest write-ups of what didn't reproduce.

Reality check

A good map, with some hype. The topics are the real core of LLM research engineering in 2026, and the projects are good practice. A few claims need caveats:

  • "Never be unemployed again" is marketing. No skill set guarantees that.
  • "Architecture is secondary, data is the secret sauce" overstates it. Data mix matters enormously, but frontier models still compete on architecture too. Module 05 credits Kimi K3's new architecture with about 2.5× scaling efficiency.
  • "Ring Attention for infinite context": ring attention spreads a long sequence across devices, so the maximum length grows with the number of GPUs. That's very long, not infinite.
  • Several projects assume real hardware (a 7B model across 4 GPUs, 50,000 reasoning traces from a frontier model). Each stage page gives a laptop/Colab-sized version first, and the full version second.
  • "Labs hire on HuggingFace repos, not degrees": public work helps a lot, but it isn't the only route.

Suggested order

  1. Foundations first: 1 → 2 → 8. You can't optimise what you can't see, and inference cost shapes everything.
  2. Then the model: 3 → 7 → 6.
  3. Then behaviour: 4 → 5 → 10 → 9.
  4. Throughout: 11 and 12 aren't stages to "finish". Do one paper reproduction and one public write-up per stage.
