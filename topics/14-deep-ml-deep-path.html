Start here (no maths needed)

A giant practice map for AI skills, drawn like a dive into the ocean. Easy topics are near the surface, and harder ones are deeper down.

Think of it like this

Imagine a video game with levels from the beach down to the deepest trench. Before you start, a quick test checks how far down you can already go, so you don't waste time on levels that are too easy. Then each day the game gives you a few new challenges at your level. It also brings back old ones just before you'd forget them.

How it works

  1. Take a short test (about 25 questions) to find your level.
  2. Each day, do a few steps: a bit of maths, some coding problems, sometimes a project.
  3. The site brings back topics you're about to forget, so they stick.
  4. Go deeper as you improve, from basic machine learning down to GPU programming.

Why robots care

The map includes reinforcement learning (how robots learn by trial and error), a section on training robots in simulation before the real world, and robot arm maths. That robotics part is still small, though, so this course fills the gap.

1. Summary

TL;DR

Deep-Path puts every problem on Deep-ML into one order: 103 concepts in 14 parts. Each concept teaches the maths it needs, then gives problems, and sometimes a project and a lab. There are two tracks:

  • Everything: 103 concepts, 2,126 steps. By the site's totals, that's 329 maths items, 1,659 problems, 87 projects and 51 labs.
  • Essentials: 69 concepts, 456 steps.

A placement test of about 25 short questions finds your frontier. After that, the daily "Today" view gives a few steps at that frontier and brings back concepts you're about to forget.

  • Deep-ML itself describes itself as a free, open-source ML challenge platform with 1,200+ problems, labs, projects and maths. There's also a paid Premium tier.
  • The depth metaphor: Sunlit (classical ML, deep learning) → Twilight (LLMs, diffusion and world models) → Midnight (RL, GNNs) → Abyss (inference engineering, CUDA/Triton, ML compilers, safety) → Trench (toolboxes: SQL, algorithms, robotics and control, maths drills).
  • It's deepest where the job market is hot: LLMs (20 concepts) and inference engineering (10 concepts) get a lot of space. Robotics and control has a single concept.
  • Some concepts are still filling in ("no project or lab yet"), and a couple of projects are marked as built but held. It's a living route.

5. Why placement + spaced repetition works

  • Placement skips material you already know and starts you at your "frontier": hard enough to learn, not so hard you stall. That's scaffolding again (Module 03).
  • Spaced repetition brings a concept back just before you'd forget it. Combined with retrieval practice (actually solving, not re-reading), it builds durable memory. These are the "desirable difficulties" from Module 03.
  • Maths, then problems, then a project mirrors how this course pairs explanation, a demo and a hands-on build.

7. Why it matters for robotics

  • Part 5's last unit, "RL systems, environments and sim-to-real", is the bridge to training robot policies in simulation.
  • Part 13's single concept (kinematics, rotations and filtering; its first problem is forward kinematics of a 2-link arm) shows the gap. Robotics needs far more: rotations and quaternions, Kalman and particle filters, control (PID, LQR, MPC), planning. These are the same gaps this course logged back in Module 02.
  • Part 4's "Video, world models and latent actions" connects to learning robot world models from video.
  • Parts 7–8 (inference, CUDA) are what put models onto robot hardware within a latency budget (Module 04).

8. Self-check

What's the difference between Deep-Path's Everything and Essentials tracks?

Everything is all 103 concepts (2,126 steps). Essentials is a 69-concept, 456-step subset.

What does the placement test do, and what happens after it?

About 25 short questions find your frontier. The daily view then gives a few steps there and brings back concepts you're about to forget.

Is the "trench" the hardest material?

No. The trench holds toolbox parts (SQL, algorithms, robotics and control, maths drills). They're supporting skills placed at the bottom of the map.

Which Deep-Path part has no matching module in this course yet?

Part 9 (ML compilers), and the SQL/algorithms toolboxes. Robotics and control is only partly covered.
