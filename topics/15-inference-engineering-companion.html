Think of it like this

Imagine a science textbook where every diagram moves, every formula has a calculator next to it, and every chapter ends with a quiz that marks itself. That's this site, for the book this course already follows in Module 13.

How it works

  1. Pick a path: "Getting Started" (about 3 hours), "Infrastructure Architect" or "Performance Optimizer" (about 4 hours each).
  2. Read a chapter, then play with its calculator. For example, see how much memory a model needs.
  3. Take the quiz at the end of the section.
  4. Use this course's pages when you want the "why" in more depth.

Why robots care

The site's memory and speed calculators answer the robot's question too: will this model fit on my robot's computer, and will it answer fast enough?

Try it

Open the site's VRAM calculator and pick a small model. Then open Module 09's KV-cache sizer and check you get similar answers.

3. Reading its benchmark critically

The site publishes a vLLM vs SGLang comparison. According to its page, it uses DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B on 2× H100 SXM with tensor parallelism 2, CUDA 12.9, and 5,980 requests across six workloads (code generation, long RAG, medium chat, prefix-cache cold/warm, 8K RAG) at several concurrency levels. It reports TPS, TTFT, p50 and p99.

  • The dates don't match: the page says "data as of 2025-12-05" and also "results collected June 2026". Check which version of each engine was tested before relying on it.
  • It's one small model on one GPU type with one parallelism setting. Rankings can flip for larger or MoE models, other GPUs, or other configs.
  • Winners vary by workload and load. On throughput (TPS), SGLang led medium chat at concurrency 100 (4,587 vs 4,432), while vLLM led medium chat at concurrency 50 and the long-RAG, code-generation and 8K-RAG rows shown. That's exactly why Module 09, Problem 18 says to benchmark your own traffic and name no universal winner.
  • It's run by the site's team, not independently replicated. Treat it as a useful starting point.

4. Why it matters for robotics

  • The VRAM and model-to-hardware tools answer "does this fit on the robot's board?". Remember that edge devices (e.g. Jetson-class) have far less memory bandwidth than data-centre GPUs. Module 09 page 06 notes the Jetson AGX Orin 64GB's 204.8 GB/s.
  • Chapter 6 (ASR, TTS, VLMs) covers the modalities a robot actually talks and sees with.
  • Latency percentiles (p99) matter more on robots than on websites: a rare slow answer is a rare late reaction.

5. Self-check

Which learning path fits an engineer tuning an existing deployment?

Performance Optimizer: quantisation, speculative decoding, KV caching and parallelism. About 4 hours, 8 steps.

Why cross-check the site's VRAM calculator with Module 09's KV-cache sizer?

Different tools make different assumptions (activation overhead, usable memory, KV precision). Explaining any gap is how you learn what drives memory.

Name two reasons not to generalise from the site's vLLM vs SGLang benchmark.

It uses one 8B model on 2× H100 with TP=2, and the winner changes by workload. The page's dates are also inconsistent.
