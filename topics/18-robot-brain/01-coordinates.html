1. The idea

💡 The idea: every square has an address

Think of a cinema ticket: "row F, seat 12". One number says how far across, one says which row. The robot's room works the same. x counts across from the left. y counts down from the top. Both start at 0.

Yes, y goes down. In maths class y goes up. On screens, rows are counted from the top. Mixing this up is the most common bug this week.

💡 The idea: a direction is a small change

"Go east" means x goes up by 1, y stays the same: the change (+1, 0). North is (0, −1), because up the screen makes y smaller. So moving is adding: from (2, 3), east gives (2 + 1, 3 + 0) = (3, 3).

Then one extra rule: work out the new square, then check it. If it's a wall, stay put.

💡 The idea: how far is it, on a grid?

A taxi in Manhattan can't drive through buildings. It goes along the streets. So the distance is the steps across plus the steps down: |x1 − x2| + |y1 − y2|. The bars mean "drop the minus sign", Python's abs().

It ignores walls, so the real route can be the same or longer, never shorter. In week 5 that's exactly what makes A* search work.

5. Real-world challenge: map your room

  1. Draw a room at home on squared paper, one square per step. Walls and furniture are #. Put (0, 0) top left, x right, y down.
  2. Label three things with their coordinates: the door, the bed, a lamp.
  3. Write a command string (N, S, E, W) from the door to one of them, and its Manhattan distance.
  4. Be the robot: follow the string one step per letter, looking only at the paper. Furniture in the way? Stay put and count a bump.
On your own: this one works alone. Record where you meant to end, where you ended, and the bumps.

Your map and the real room won't quite agree: your steps aren't all the same size. Robots drift too. That's why week 2 adds sensors.

6 + 5 = 11. Never shorter: walls can only add steps. (In week 5 you'll find the real shortest route is exactly 11.)

Why should safe_move call move instead of doing its own maths?

The maths lives in one place. Fix move and everything that uses it is fixed too.

Want to know more?

Common mistakes
  • y the wrong way. North is y − 1.
  • Reading the grid as ROOM[x][y]. It's ROOM[y][x]: row first. is_free handles this, so use it.
  • Forgetting abs(). manhattan((4, 5), (1, 1)) gives −7. The checker says use abs().
  • Returning a list. [3, 3] == (3, 3) is False. Use round brackets.
