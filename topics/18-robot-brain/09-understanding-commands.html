Start here

To understand a command, the robot answers two questions: what do you want (the intent), and which thing (the slot). It learns the first by counting words in examples.

Think of sorting post without opening it. "Invoice" goes on the bills pile, "birthday" on the invitations pile. You've never seen this letter before, but its words give it away. Our robot sorts commands into 4 piles: put, go, find, stop.

Intent
What the person wants: put, go, find or stop.
Slot
The detail: which object, like "red block".
Training set / test set
Examples to learn from (34) and different ones kept aside to check (12).
Accuracy
The fraction of test examples it gets right.

1. The idea

💡 The idea: every word votes

Keyword rules break fast. "get rid of the red block" has no "put". "where did the blue one go" has "go", but it's a question. So instead, count how often each word appears in each pile's examples. Here are real counts from the 34 training commands:

"bin" votes put. "where" votes find. "the" turns up almost everywhere, so its vote barely matters. To score a command, every word casts its vote for every pile, and the pile with the strongest total wins. This is called Naive Bayes. "Naive" because it treats each word as a separate clue, ignoring word order.

💡 Two small fixes

Fair shares. Put examples have 61 words in total and stop examples only 15. So we use each word's share of its pile, not the raw count.

Never zero. "halt" never appears in put examples. If a word could score exactly 0, one new word would wipe out all the other clues. So we add 1 to every count. And because multiplying many tiny chances gives numbers too small for the computer, we add their logs instead. Every score ends up negative, and the one closest to 0 wins.

Two gaps in my_robot.py:

6. Check yourself

What's the difference between an intent and a slot?

The intent is what you want (put, go, find, stop). The slot is the detail (which block).

Why add 1 to every count?

So one word never seen with an intent can't make its score 0 and wipe out every other clue.

Predict: extract_object("go to the red block, not the blue one")?

"red block", the first colour. It can't understand "not".

Predict: what does it say for "hello robot"?

stop. It must always pick one of its 4 piles. It has no way to say "I don't know".

7. Want to know more?

The maths, with real numbers

Add-one smoothing: P(word in intent) = (count + 1) / (all words in intent + 52), where 52 is the vocabulary size. Scoring "stop" by hand:

put:   10/34 × (0 + 1)/(61 + 52)  =  0.294 × 0.00885  =  0.0026
stop:   8/34 × (6 + 1)/(15 + 52)  =  0.235 × 0.1045   =  0.0246    ← about 10× bigger

Logs turn those into −5.95 and −3.71. Three facts: the log of a number between 0 and 1 is negative; a smaller number gives a more negative log; and log(a × b) = log(a) + log(b), so we add instead of multiply.

             prior  where's   the    green   one     total

go    (log)  −1.45   −4.56   −2.37   −3.47  −3.47   −15.31
find  (log)  −1.45   −4.61   −2.41   −3.51  −3.51   −15.47
stop  (log)  −1.45   −4.20   −4.20   −4.20  −4.20   −18.27
