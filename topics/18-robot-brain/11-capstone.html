Start here

A robot is lots of small ideas passing messages to each other. The hard part is making the hand-offs work, then measuring the whole thing honestly.

Think of a restaurant: the waiter writes the order, the chef checks it's on the menu, the cooks plan and cook, and a critic tastes ten dishes and writes "9 out of 10, the soup was cold". No single person is the restaurant.

Pipeline
A chain of steps where each one's output is the next one's input.
Test set
A fixed list of tasks, with the right answers, to score the whole robot.
Failure analysis
Looking at each failed test to find out why.

1. The pipeline

💡 The idea

Someone types "put the blue block in the bin". The message flows through your earlier weeks, one hand-off at a time. Each piece agreed on the shape of what it passes on, so they snap together.

💡 Follow the cat

A cat sits at (4, 1), and it isn't on the map. A* plans the shortest route to the blue block, straight through the cat. At (3, 1) the stop-neuron sees something 0.5 m ahead and brakes. The robot marks (4, 1) as blocked and asks A* again. It has to back up, because (3, 2) is a wall:

Remembering the cat is what turns a stop into a replan. Without it, A* would plan the same route again, and again (it gives up after 5 tries).

💡 Why it fails (the honest part)

"find the green block" binned it. Week 7's word counter scored "put" just above "find" (a gap of only 0.09), because "the green block" appears in lots of "put" examples. Checking that nothing else changed is what caught it.


"make me a sandwich" became "stop". The "stop" examples are short (15 words in total), so "stop" gives the biggest share to words it has never seen: 1 in 67, against 1 in 113 for "put". Four strange words and "stop" wins. The word counter always picks a winner; it can't say "I don't know". That's the stretch below.

5. Real-world challenge: be Robo-Helper


🌍 Real-world challenge

With a family member: draw a room of your home on squared paper with a box as the bin and three small objects as blocks. They write 10 commands on slips, including two impossible ones. For each one: check it against the rules (put / go / find / stop, only your three objects), plan the route on paper, then walk it. They may quietly drop a cushion on your route (the cat): stop, mark it, replan. Score yourself, and write one line on why for each failure.


On your own: write the 10 slips and leave them in a jar overnight. For the cat, roll a die before each walk: on a 1 or 2, drop the cushion two steps ahead of you halfway along.





6. Check yourself


Why does every test use a fresh robot?
So each test starts from the same room. Otherwise the order of the tests would change the score.


On one robot, "put the red block in the bin" twice. What does the second call say?
"The red block is already in the bin.", with ok True. It doesn't move.


"go to the moon" is nonsense too. Why does it pass (get refused)?
It's read as "go", but there's no colour, so the object is None, and "go" needs an object. Refused by luck, not understanding.


The robot scored 83%. Is it 83% good at real life?
No. It's 83% on 12 hand-picked commands in one room with one cat. A score is only as honest as its test set.
