Attention: how Transformers read

Your tiny LM reads a command by averaging all its words, once. Real LLMs choose which words to look at, fresh for every word they write. That's attention. You write one function.

Start here

Averaging gives every word the same vote at every step. Attention lets the model ask a question at each step and listen hardest to the words that answer it.

Think of a teacher asking the class a question. Everyone could shout at once (averaging). Or the teacher can listen hardest to the pupils who know the answer, and a different question picks out different pupils.

Query
The question at this step: the embedding of the word just written.
Key
What each command word offers: its embedding.
Dot product
Multiply two lists position by position and add up. Big means "these match".
Attention weights
How much it listens to each word. They add up to 1.

1. The idea

💡 The idea: spotlights on words

At every step the model shines a spotlight on the command's words. Brighter = more weight. Real weights from the trained attention model for "put the red and green blocks away":

Choosing the action, the light is spread out. After "put", "red" grabs 0.77 of it. Nobody told it to do this: training found that it helps. If every weight were equal, you'd get the old average back.

💡 How the spotlight is aimed
  1. Score every word: the dot product of its key with the query, divided by √E.
  2. Softmax the scores into weights (your softmax from week 8).
  3. Mix: each word's embedding times its weight, added up. That's the context.

Picture each word's numbers as an arrow. The dot product is big when two arrows point the same way. Here with tiny 2-number words, and the query (purple band) as the question:

Same words, different question, different focus. (By hand: scores ÷ 1.414 are put 0, red 1.414, the 0.354, then softmax.)

4. Does it help?

A little on average (92% against 89%), and much steadier: 92% on every seed, while averaging swings from 80% to 94%. On seed 1, averaging wins. On the robot's 12-task exam both score 8/12. Small experiments wobble, so use several seeds and only believe a difference that shows up every time.

5. Check yourself

If every attention weight is equal, what is the context?

The plain average: exactly what week 8's read gives.

Does "away block red the put" give a different plan from "put the red block away"?

No, ['put', 'red'] for both. A word's embedding is the same wherever it sits. Real Transformers add position information.

92% every seed against 92%, 94%, 80%. Which is better?

Attention on average, and steadier. But one run on one seed could have told you either story.

6. Want to know more?

The clever step, and the new mistakes

In "throw out all of them except the blue one", after writing red it looks hardest at "blue" (0.39), the colour to leave out, together with "all" (0.37), and writes green. With seed 0, attention fixes "please put everything in the bin" and "stop the red block". But for "go to the green block" it writes just go, which words_to_plan rejects.

What attention does NOT fix
  • Word order. "go to the blue block not the red one" gives ['go', 'red', 'go'], rejected. The averaging model's ['go', 'blue'] was right only by luck.
  • Unknown words. "where's" has no key, so for "where's the green one?" attention looks at "the green one" and writes put green, which would bin the block. The averaging model wrote just put, which was rejected: accidentally safer.
