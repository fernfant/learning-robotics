Time a brain many times. Judge it by its slow answers, not its average. And count what each answer costs.

Robo-Helper rolls at 1 metre a second. A cat steps out 1 metre ahead. You shout "stop!". If the brain thinks for 0.01 s, the robot moves 1 cm. If it thinks for 2 s, it rolls 2 m, straight through where the cat was. Same robot, same right answer. Only the time is different.

Inference
Using a trained brain to get an answer. It happens on every command.
Latency
How long one answer takes.
p50
The middle time once you sort them: "the usual time".
p95
95 out of 100 answers are faster than this: "the bad-day time".
Token
A chunk of text an AI reads or writes: a short word, or part of a long one.

1. The idea

💡 The idea: a stopwatch, and how far you roll

While the brain thinks, the wheels keep turning. So every thinking time is also a distance: distance = seconds × speed.

Our budget: react to "stop!" within 0.2 seconds, about as fast as a person.

💡 The idea: judge by the slow answers

Think of a school bus. Most days it's 2 minutes late; one day in twenty it's 15. If you have an exam, you plan for the bad day. Here are 10 practice timings of an imaginary internet brain (made-up numbers, so you can work with a pen): 1.2 1.1 1.3 1.2 4.8 1.1 1.2 1.4 1.2 1.3. Each dot is one answer:

Sorted: 1.1 1.1 1.2 1.2 1.2 1.2 1.3 1.3 1.4 4.8. p50 is position ceil(0.5 × 10) − 1 = 4 → 1.2 s. p95 is position ceil(0.95 × 10) − 1 = 9 → 4.8 s. The mean, 1.58 s, describes neither. A robot hearing 100 commands a day gets about 5 answers slower than its p95. So the rule is: a brain fits the budget only if its p95 fits.

💡 The idea: tokens, and the price tag

A big AI writes its answer one token at a time and can send each one as soon as it's ready (streaming). That splits the wait in two:

Internet models charge per million tokens. For claude-opus-5-5 the file uses $4 per million in and $20 per million out. One command with 1,000 tokens in and 200 out: 1,000 ÷ 1,000,000 × $4 + 200 ÷ 1,000,000 × $20 = $0.008. Why 1,000 tokens for an 8-word command? The rules and the room description get sent with every request.

5. Real-world challenge: your own p50 and p95

🌍 Real-world challenge

You are a brain too. A friend holds a 30 cm ruler with 0 at the bottom between your open finger and thumb, and drops it without warning. Note where you catch it. Do it 20 times, then turn each distance into a time:

Caught at5 cm10 cm15 cm20 cm25 cm30 cm
Time0.10 s0.14 s0.18 s0.20 s0.23 s0.25 s

Sort your 20 times. p50 is position 9 (the 10th smallest), p95 is position 18. Now do 20 more while counting backwards from 100 in sevens. Which moves more, p50 or p95? Do you fit the 0.2 s budget?

On your own: start a phone stopwatch and try to stop it at exactly 1.00 s without looking. Do it 20 times, write down how far off you were each time, and find p50 and p95. Then do 20 more while counting backwards.

Most people find distraction barely moves the usual time but stretches the slow tail, just like a busy computer.

6. Check yourself

What does summarize([0.2, 0.1, 0.1, 0.3, 0.1]) return?

Sorted: [0.1, 0.1, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3]. p50: ceil(2.5) − 1 = 2 → 0.1. p95: ceil(4.75) − 1 = 4 → 0.3. Mean: 0.8 ÷ 5 = 0.16. The usual answer is fast, but the slow one fails a 0.2 s budget.

A brain has p50 = 0.05 s and p95 = 0.4 s. Can it handle "stop!"?

No. 1 in 20 answers takes longer than 0.2 s, and the budget is judged on p95. A long tail usually means it sometimes waits on something: the network, a busy server, other programs.

500 tokens in and 500 out. What does it cost?

In: 500 ÷ 1,000,000 × $4 = $0.002. Out: 500 ÷ 1,000,000 × $20 = $0.01. Total $0.012. Writing costs 5× more than reading.

Why must the stop reflex stay on the robot?

Because an internet brain's p95 is far over 0.2 s, and one network hiccup can make it much worse. Real robots have layers: fast reflexes on board, a small local brain, and a big far-away brain that plans while the reflexes keep watch.
