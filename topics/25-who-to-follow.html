Who to follow in robot learning: 12 researchers, what each one builds, and where it meets our course

A list of names is only useful if you know what each person works on. This page turns a “follow these 12” post into a map of robot learning: six big questions, who is working on each, their best-known papers, and the Robo-Helper week where a 12-year-old has already met the same idea.

Source: Ilir Aliu (@IlirAliu_), post on X, 15 Nov 2026 · post. The post gives only handles and affiliations. Everything else on this page (roles, papers, links) we looked up on each person's own homepage, lab page or arXiv on 16 Nov 2026, and every link was checked. Where we couldn't confirm something, we say so.

For Robo-Helper builders (ages 12+): Read “Start here”, then play the matching game. The profiles have one kid-sized sentence each and a video-friendly project page to look at with a grown-up. Back to the course.

Start here (no maths needed)

Every clever trick in Robo-Helper was first worked out by a real person, wrote up as a paper, and shared. These 12 people are inventing the next tricks right now.

Think of it like this

  • Robot learning is like a giant science fair. Each scientist has a stand with one big question: “Can a robot learn by watching?”, “Can it learn by practising?”, “Can it follow what I say?”
  • You already built small versions of most of these stands. Your robot learned from data, practised by trial and error, and followed spoken-style commands.
  • Following a scientist just means checking their stand every so often to see what they tried next.

What they make robots do

  1. Pick up any random object from a messy pile, like winning the claw game every time.
  2. Fly a drone through a race course faster than the world champion.
  3. Tidy your room the way you like it.
  4. Do a chore after watching a video of a person doing it.
  5. Walk, jump and balance like an athlete, after practising in a video-game world first.

Why robots care

Nobody writes every rule for a robot by hand any more. These people work out how robots can learn the rules, which is exactly what Robo-Helper does on a small scale.

Words to know

Paper
A write-up of an experiment: what they tried, what happened, with numbers.
Simulation
A video-game version of the world where a robot can practise without breaking anything.
Sim-to-real
Taking what a robot learned in the game world and making it work on a real robot.
World model
A robot's “imagination”: a way to guess what will happen before it acts.
Humanoid
A robot shaped like a person, with two legs and two arms.

1. Summary

TL;DR

Twelve researchers who post about their own work: eight professors, three PhD students (one also works at Apptronik) and one researcher at Physical Intelligence. Several professors also hold company roles. Together they cover most of modern robot learning: grasping and manipulation, robots that follow language (including OpenVLA, the open vision-language-action model), learning from human video, reinforcement learning on real robots, agile legs, humanoids and drones, and fast simulators and world models. The post is a starting list, not a curriculum. Its value comes from knowing which question each person is answering.

2. Why this matters for robotics

Track: Robotics. This is the research frontier that our robotics modules point towards. It reaches back to the LLM side through two people in particular: Wenlong Huang showed that language models can plan robot tasks, and Siddharth Karamcheti co-led OpenVLA, which puts a vision-language model in charge of a robot arm (see Module 21 on VLAs and Module 23, month 6).

  • It answers “who invented this?” for ideas already in the course: Robo-Helper's trial-and-error side quest is a toy version of the RL these labs run on real robots, and week 9's 2%-bigger wheel is a tiny sim-to-real gap.
  • It shows where the field is going. Three trends run through the list: learning from human video instead of expensive robot data, learned simulators (“world models”), and getting simulation-trained skills to survive real hardware.
  • It gives open tools to try next: ManiSkill (simulation), OpenVLA (a VLA you can download), SERL (real-robot RL), TidyBot++ (an open mobile robot), DROID and RoboSet (datasets).

4. Deep dive: the 12 profiles

Roles were checked on 16 Nov 2026. Each profile lists three signature works (title, year, link) and what you can download.

Ken Goldberg @Ken_Goldberg

Distinguished Professor, UC Berkeley (IEOR, EECS, Art Practice); AUTOLab; co-founder and Chief Scientist of Ambi Robotics · homepage

Robot grasping and handling of messy real-world objects, often by training on large amounts of synthetic data. Also surgical robots and cloud robotics.

  • Dex-Net 2.0 (RSS 2017): a grasping network trained only on 6.7 million synthetic examples grasps objects it has never seen.
  • Learning ambidextrous robot grasping policies (Dex-Net 4.0, Science Robotics 2019): chooses between suction and a gripper; over 95% reliable, 300+ picks per hour on bins.
  • FogROS2 (2022): hands a robot's heavy computing to the cloud.

Try: Dex-Net code and data. For a kid: the claw game, but it wins almost every time.

Guanya Shi @GuanyaShi

Assistant Professor, Robotics Institute, CMU; LeCAR Lab; Amazon Scholar · homepage

Combines learning with control theory for agile, adaptive robots. Recent work is whole-body humanoids, sim-to-real transfer, and learning from human motion.

  • Neural-Fly (Science Robotics 2022): drones adapt within seconds to fly steadily in strong wind.
  • ASAP (RSS 2025): learns the gap between simulated and real physics so humanoids can do athletic moves.
  • OmniH2O (CoRL 2024): a person drives a humanoid's whole body remotely, and the robot learns from that data.

Try: the LeCAR Lab repos (human2humanoid, ASAP, dial-mpc). For a kid: humanoids and drones that move like athletes.

Jeannette Bohg @leto__jean

Associate Professor of Computer Science, Stanford; Interactive Perception and Robot Learning Lab · homepage

Grasping and manipulation across robotics, ML and vision. Her central idea: robots understand the world better by touching and moving things, not just looking.

  • Interactive Perception (IEEE T-RO 2017): the survey that defined perceiving through action.
  • TidyBot (IROS 2023): an LLM learns one person's tidying preferences from a few examples; 85% of objects put away correctly in real tests.
  • TidyBot++ (CoRL 2024): an open mobile manipulator for robot learning, about $5–6k to build.

Try: TidyBot++ hardware and code. TidyBot is the grown-up version of week 16, learning what people prefer. For a kid: a robot that tidies your room the way you like it.

Wenlong Huang @wenlong_huang

Final-year PhD student, Stanford Vision and Learning Lab (advised by Fei-Fei Li) · homepage

Uses language and vision-language models to help robots reason and plan, aiming for robots that generalise across tasks, places and bodies without retraining.

  • Language Models as Zero-Shot Planners (ICML 2022): LLMs already know enough to break everyday tasks into steps.
  • VoxPoser (CoRL 2023): language and vision models draw 3D “go here, avoid there” maps that a robot follows, with no task-specific training. It's a cousin of the potential fields in Module 22.
  • ReKep (CoRL 2024): describes tasks as rules about key points on objects, so the robot can plan multi-step manipulation.

For a kid: tell the robot what to do in plain English, and it works out how.

Yunzhu Li @YunzhuLiYZ

Assistant Professor of Computer Science, Columbia; RoboPIL lab; also leads robotics at World Labs · homepage

Learned world models that predict how objects, including squishy and deformable ones, move when a robot acts; combines vision, touch, audio and language.

  • Learning Particle Dynamics (ICLR 2019): a learned particle simulator for solids, foam and liquids.
  • RoboEXP (CoRL 2024): the robot opens drawers and doors to explore and maps what's hidden where.
  • 3D-ViTac (CoRL 2024): cheap touch sensors plus vision for handling fragile objects.

For a kid: giving robots an imagination, so they can guess what dough or water will do before touching it.

Abhishek Gupta @abhishekunique7

Assistant Professor, Allen School, University of Washington; WEIRD Lab · homepage

Reinforcement learning that lets robots learn and keep improving in real homes and offices, starting from models pretrained on video, simulation and generated data.

  • SERL (ICRA 2024): an open toolkit for real robots to learn skills by trial and error in hours.
  • Unified World Models (RSS 2025, Best Paper): one model predicts video and chooses actions, so it can learn from videos with no action labels.
  • Steering Your Diffusion Policy with Latent Space RL (CoRL 2025): improves a pretrained robot policy with RL without retraining it.

For a kid: robots that get better by practising, like you at a video game.

Siddharth Karamcheti @siddkaramcheti

Assistant Professor of Interactive Computing, Georgia Tech; Autonomy Research Lead, Walden Robotics; Stanford PhD 2025 · homepage

Foundation models that help robots see and act, and robots that keep learning from the people they work with.

  • OpenVLA (CoRL 2024): an open 7B vision-language-action model, camera image + instruction → arm actions. A standard baseline.
  • DROID (RSS 2024): a large robot manipulation dataset collected in many real homes and labs.
  • Vocal Sandbox (CoRL 2024): people teach a robot new skills by talking and showing while working together. Related to Module 03, shared autonomy.

Try: OpenVLA, Prismatic VLMs. For a kid: robot brains that follow spoken instructions and learn tricks from people.

Homanga Bharadhwaj @mangahomanga

Assistant Professor of Computer Science, Johns Hopkins; Brains, Bots, and Behavior Lab · homepage

Robots doing everyday chores in messy homes and kitchens, largely by learning to predict what happens next in videos of people.

  • Track2Act (ECCV 2024): predicts how points in a scene will move, learned from internet video, so the robot can do unseen tasks.
  • Gen2Act (CoRL 2025): generates a video of a person doing the task, then the robot copies it.
  • RoboAgent (ICRA 2024): many manipulation skills from a small amount of robot data.

Try: the RoboSet dataset. For a kid: robots that learn chores by watching videos of people.

Chenhao Li @breadli428

Doctoral Fellow, ETH AI Center (advised by Marco Hutter and Andreas Krause); Student Researcher, Gemini Robotics at Google DeepMind since April 2026 · homepage

Learning methods for complex, agile skills: reinforcement learning, world models (learned simulators) and VLAs.

  • Robotic World Model (NeurIPS 2025 workshop, Outstanding Paper): a neural network that learns to be the simulator robots train in.
  • Uncertainty-Aware Robotic World Model (2026): trains real robots from logged data only, no new trial and error.
  • FLD: Fourier Latent Dynamics (ICLR 2024): packs movements like gaits into a compact form robots can learn and blend. Its maths is the Fourier idea in Module 21.

Try: FLD code. For a kid: legged robots practise in a learned imagination before trying for real.

Davide Scaramuzza @davsca1

Professor, Department of Informatics, University of Zurich; Director, Robotics and Perception Group · homepage

Fast, agile, camera-based navigation for drones and mobile robots; known for event cameras, which react to change with very little delay.

  • Champion-level drone racing using deep reinforcement learning (Nature 2023): the Swift drone beat human world-champion pilots.
  • Low-latency automotive vision with event cameras (Nature 2024): faster, lower-power perception for cars.
  • Learning High-Speed Flight in the Wild (Science Robotics 2021): fast flight through forests with only onboard sensing.

Try: uzh-rpg code, the DSEC event-camera dataset. For a kid: drones that fly a race course faster than the best human pilots.

Stone Tao @Stone_Tao

Physical Intelligence, “simulation and research” per his GitHub bio (exact title not confirmed); previously a UC San Diego PhD student with Hao Su · GitHub

Fast, GPU-parallel robot simulation and data-efficient RL, and getting simulation-trained skills onto real robots.

  • ManiSkill3 (2024): a very fast simulator that trains robot arms on many tasks at once.
  • Reverse Forward Curriculum Learning (ICLR 2024): hard tasks from only a few demonstrations.
  • SILO (2026): cable routing learned in simulation, then done on real hardware.

Try: ManiSkill, lerobot-sim2real. For a kid: super-fast game worlds where robots practise thousands of times.

Kyle Morgenstein @KyleMorgenstein

Roboticist at Apptronik since June 2025 (exact title not confirmed); PhD student, Human Centered Robotics Lab, UT Austin · profile

Reinforcement learning for legged and humanoid robots, especially the practical tricks that make simulation-trained policies work on real hardware.

  • Too Much of a Good Thing: When sim2real Efforts Impede Policy Learning (2026): too much effort on a realistic simulator can hold learning back; proposes “sim2sim2real”.
  • Deployment-ready RL: pitfalls, lessons and best practices (talk, Aug 2025): motor gains, reward design, and why measuring the real robot (system identification) matters most.
  • Learning Contact-based Navigation in Crowds (HRI 2023 workshop): a robot gently pushes through crowds instead of freezing.

For a kid: humanoids that learn to walk safely by practising in a simulation first. His “measure the real robot” advice is week 9 in one line: with a left wheel 2% bigger than the code assumes, the robot thinks it has arrived but is really 0.63 m away.

6. A reading order that follows the course

Start with project pages and videos, not PDFs. One per stage of Robo-Helper:

  1. After week 2 (learning from data): Track2Act. Watch how points in a video predict a robot's motion.
  2. After the trial-and-error side quest: the Swift drone race. Same idea as your Q-table, scaled up to a real drone.
  3. After week 7 (understanding commands): VoxPoser, then OpenVLA. Words become a map; then words become motion directly.
  4. After week 9 (wheels and PID): Morgenstein's deployment-ready RL talk and Shi's ASAP. Both are about the sim-to-real gap you measured.
  5. After week 16 (preferences): TidyBot. Learning what one person wants, on a real robot.
  6. Grown-up, hands-on: install ManiSkill and run one task, then read Gupta's SERL to see what changes on hardware.

8. Playbook

  1. Make an X list (not a follow-spree) with the 12, so their posts stay in one place and out of the main feed.
  2. Sort by question. Pick the one of the six questions you care about most and follow those 3–5 people closely.
  3. Read project page → video → abstract → figures → method, in that order. Stop when you've got the idea.
  4. Run one thing: ManiSkill, OpenVLA or SERL. Running code teaches more than ten threads.
  5. For the 12-year-old: play the matching game, then watch one project video a week with a grown-up, matched to the week they've finished (see the reading order).
  6. Write it down as a log entry here: what the paper did, one number, and which week it connects to.

9. Self-check

Why do so many people on this list work on learning from human video?

Robot data is slow and expensive to collect (one robot, one demo at a time). Human video is everywhere. Track2Act, Gen2Act and Unified World Models all find ways to learn robot actions from it.

What's the difference between VoxPoser and OpenVLA?

VoxPoser uses existing language and vision models to draw a 3D map of where to go and what to avoid, and a planner follows it, with no robot training. OpenVLA is trained end-to-end on robot data to output actions directly from an image and an instruction.

What's a world model, and who on the list builds them?

A learned model that predicts what happens next when the robot acts, so it can plan or practise in its “imagination”. Yunzhu Li (particles, deformable objects), Chenhao Li (Robotic World Model for legged robots) and Gupta (Unified World Models).

Morgenstein says measuring the real robot matters most. Where did you see that in Robo-Helper?

Week 9: with a left wheel 2% bigger than the code assumed, the robot thought it had arrived but was really 0.63 m away. Knowing the true wheel size (system identification) fixes it better than any controller tuning.

Why did Dex-Net train on synthetic data?

Millions of labelled grasps are cheap to generate in simulation and impossible to collect by hand. Dex-Net 2.0 used 6.7 million and still worked on real, unseen objects.

What does an event camera do differently?

Instead of whole frames at a fixed rate, each pixel reports changes as they happen, with very low delay. That helps fast drones and cars react sooner.

10. Glossary & further reading

TermMeaning
CoRLConference on Robot Learning, the main yearly conference for this field.
VLAVision-language-action model: image + instruction in, robot actions out.
Sim-to-realTransferring a skill learned in simulation to a real robot.
System identificationMeasuring a real robot's physical parameters (mass, friction, wheel size) so models match it.
World modelA learned predictor of what happens next, used to plan or practise.
TeleoperationA person controlling a robot remotely, often to collect training data.
Diffusion policyA policy that generates actions the way image diffusion generates pictures.
Event cameraA sensor whose pixels report brightness changes as they happen.
Zero-shotDoing a task with no examples of that task in training.
